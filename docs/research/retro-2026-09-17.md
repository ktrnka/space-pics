# Space pics build day: retrospective

**Date**: 2026-09-17. **Repo**: ktrnka/space-pics. Two drafts written independently (Keith's and Claude's), then merged.
The full timeline is in `NOTES.md`.

## Summary

Goal: in one time-boxed day, ship a site showing a recent space image daily from several sources, with RSS and an
unattended job, and find out whether an agent-heavy day is a real productivity jump. Shipped the must-do by 10:33; the
rest of the day went to an explorer over all the data, a subject-of-the-day digest with instrument cards, eleven
sources, and a wigglegram. Keith's verdict on the premise: energised, some legitimately cool things, but it felt like
normal development with more subagents rather than a massive boost.

## What went well

- The wigglegram, and the pivot to subject pages: the two things that felt genuinely cool, both born from Keith
  looking at the explorer rather than from the plan.
- Subagents, used offline-first: fetch once, then hand agents saved data with a no-network rule and disjoint file sets.
  Seven extractors, 47 instrument cards, three reviews, no collisions, no load on the sources.
- The agent responded well to direction on maintenance cost and extensibility.
- Agentic use of the GitHub CLI: dispatching runs, watching them, reading logs, setting the secret. Keith never had to
  babysit Actions.
- The lunch walk reset Keith's view; the noon reframe came right after it. The unattended lunch hour was also the
  most experimental hour of the day.

## What didn't go well

- Most of the day was web search and data engineering, work Keith could have done himself, if slower. The image
  processing never started. Directing toward useful over interesting, plus a plan that put the pipeline first, kept
  the interesting work on the board for day 2.
- Keith carried worry about server etiquette (bad experiences with other agents). The load plan was stated once, at
  noon, when it should have preceded every fetch round.
- Keith meant to run his own concurrent agents and never did: no ready prompts, and he was the review bottleneck.
- No deep space images made a post; the rotation is Sun, Mars, Earth and deep space lives only in the explorer.
- Maybe 30 minutes too long on idea triage at the start: about 15 ideas evaluated, 5 in depth, then one picked.
  The build-plan forks in the session itself were quick (a minute or two each).
- (Claude's column) Deploys were silently broken for four hours because run status was checked instead of the served
  page; three predictions lost to Keith's eyes (calibration frames, gzip, the wigglegram metric); two late-caught
  URL-shape hazards; two push races with the bot. Keith experienced these as blips; they're calibration for the agent.

## On the mind, not yet good or bad

- Keith read no code all day (68 commits). Two questions, unresolved: could reviewing just the right pieces (the
  contracts in `models.py` and `sources/base.py`, the digest recipes) have made the day faster or caught late surprises
  like the render-URL filename bug? And were learning opportunities missed, especially in the image-processing
  experiments, which ran in a scratch directory during lunch and reached him as results, not methods?
- Long embargoes (Solar Orbiter 20 months, ESA archives 6 to 12 months) forced a rethink of what "recent" can mean.
- Feedback speed: Keith's review comments were the highest-value input all day, so the cost was latency, not wording.

## Improvements to consider

1. **Make concurrency cheap for Keith.** Before the next build day, a short list of independent side quests with
   ready-to-paste prompts (a SUVI composite, Mass Effect styled CSS, hours of source research), and the agent offers
   one at every natural pause: "while you review, launch this?"
2. **Reserve time for interesting work regardless of plumbing state.** Start image processing by early afternoon,
   and give the agent unattended blocks with an explicit mandate to run experiments; the lunch hour showed what that
   produces.
3. **State the load plan before every fetch round, and verify deploys from the served page.** Counts, pauses, and
   hosts up front so Keith doesn't have to worry; a cache-busted fetch of the page after every deploy, never job
   status alone.

Also worth doing before day 2: a short guided tour of the code for Keith (three files: `digest.py`, `pipeline.py`,
one source adapter), so the maintenance-cost direction he gave can be checked against what was built.
