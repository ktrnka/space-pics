*ChemCam RMI (Remote Micro-Imager)* is short for ChemCam Remote Micro-Imager. A 1024x1024-pixel monochrome CCD behind a 110mm telescope with a 700mm effective focal length, giving a narrow field of view of about 20 milliradians - roughly 20cm across at 10 meters, resolving objects about 1mm across at that same distance. A small circular telescopic view inset in a black square frame, always greyscale. Look for pairs of near-identical frames - taken before and after a LIBS burn, where the only difference is a barely visible dark pit - or a full 1024x1024 frame used for distant-terrain scouting rather than laser targeting.

## HiRISE on Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter

<img src="{{ '/assets/img/2026-11-25/hirise-ESP_077505_2650.jpg' | absolute_url }}" alt="Defrosting Dunes">

<p class="caption">2026-11-25 15:37 UTC<br>
<a href="https://uahirise.org/hipod/ESP_077505_2650">Original</a> · NASA/JPL-Caltech/University of Arizona</p>

*HiRISE* is short for High Resolution Imaging Science Experiment. From MRO's roughly 300km orbit, HiRISE resolves about 0.3 meters per pixel - individual boulders and rover tracks are visible. It scans a long strip as the spacecraft flies overhead; the full-width detector is black and white, but a narrower strip down the center is also imaged in three color bands (blue-green 400-600nm, red 550-850nm, near-infrared 800-1000nm). A full frame can run to about 20,000 x 126,000 pixels. A top-down, mostly greyscale strip of terrain, sharp enough at full resolution to show rocks, dunes and even rover hardware from orbit; color, when present, only runs down the central portion of the frame. What's published here is normally a cropped, downsized detail rather than a full multi-gigapixel swath.
