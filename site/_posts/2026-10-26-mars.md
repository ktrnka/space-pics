*Navcam* is short for Navigation Camera. It's a normal wide-angle perspective, not a fisheye. The frames here are the raw feed's 1200-pixel-wide versions of a 5120 x 3840 sensor, so fine detail is lost; the full-resolution products are on the NASA raw-images site. Expect a wide color landscape, often one tile of a larger panorama; frames sharing a sequence id were taken together as a mosaic.

## SuperCam RMI (Remote Micro-Imager) on Perseverance rover

<img src="{{ '/assets/img/2026-10-26/perseverance-LRE_2020_0846259715_241ECM_N0920000SCAM01020_0010I6J.jpg' | absolute_url }}" alt="Perseverance SUPERCAM_RMI, sol 2020">

<a href="{{ '/debug/perseverance.html#SCAM01020' | relative_url }}">All frames of this sequence</a> · <a href="https://mars.nasa.gov/mars2020/multimedia/raw-images/LRE_2020_0846259715_241ECM_N0920000SCAM01020_0010I6J">Original</a> · NASA/JPL-Caltech</p>

*SuperCam RMI (Remote Micro-Imager)* is short for SuperCam Remote Micro-Imager. The RMI shares SuperCam's 110mm-aperture, 563mm-focal-length telescope, recording 2048x2048-pixel color images through a narrow circular field of view of about 19 milliradians (roughly 1 degree) - resolving detail near 0.24mm per pixel at 3 meters, but also able to pick out crater rims and cliffs kilometers away. A frame shows a small circle of telescopic detail inset in a black square, not a full rectangular photo - that circle is the whole field of view, so zoom in rather than expecting it to fill the frame. Wider scenes are mosaics stitched from several such tiles.

*Mastcam-Z* is short for Mast Camera Zoom. Each eye has an 8-position filter wheel behind the zoom lens, producing 1600x1200-pixel frames. Position 0 (L0/R0) is a Bayer color filter for ordinary RGB images. Positions 1-6 are narrowband science filters: the left eye at 800, 754, 677, 605, 528 and 442nm, the right eye at 800, 866, 910, 939, 978 and 1022nm, spanning visible light into the near infrared. Position 7 on each eye is a neutral-density filter for looking directly at the sun; those frames are excluded from this feed. Most raw frames look like an ordinary color landscape photo (the L0/R0 Bayer filter). A narrowband frame (filter name like ZCAM_L5_528NM) is instead a flat greyscale image of a single wavelength - that's expected, not a bug; several such frames sharing a sequence ID are meant to be stacked later into a false-color composite. Frames sharing a sequence ID can also just be tiles of a wider mosaic or panorama.
