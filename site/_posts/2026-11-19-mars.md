## HiRISE on Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter

<img src="{{ '/assets/img/2026-11-19/hirise-ESP_077490_2155.jpg' | absolute_url }}" alt="Troughs and Ridges">

<p class="caption">2026-11-19 15:37 UTC<br>
<a href="https://uahirise.org/hipod/ESP_077490_2155">Original</a> · NASA/JPL-Caltech/University of Arizona</p>

*HiRISE* is short for High Resolution Imaging Science Experiment. From MRO's roughly 300km orbit, HiRISE resolves about 0.3 meters per pixel - individual boulders and rover tracks are visible. It scans a long strip as the spacecraft flies overhead; the full-width detector is black and white, but a narrower strip down the center is also imaged in three color bands (blue-green 400-600nm, red 550-850nm, near-infrared 800-1000nm). A full frame can run to about 20,000 x 126,000 pixels. A top-down, mostly greyscale strip of terrain, sharp enough at full resolution to show rocks, dunes and even rover hardware from orbit; color, when present, only runs down the central portion of the frame. What's published here is normally a cropped, downsized detail rather than a full multi-gigapixel swath.
