4 recent images of Mars from Curiosity, MRO, Perseverance.


## Navcam (NAVCAM_LEFT) on Perseverance rover

<img src="{{ '/assets/img/2026-09-17/perseverance-NLG_1982_0842901206_380ECM_N0910970NCAM00528_00_2I4J.jpg' | absolute_url }}" alt="Perseverance NAVCAM_LEFT, sol 1982">

*Perseverance NAVCAM_LEFT, sol 1982.* Sol (Mars day of the mission) 1982. Product type (ECM = processed image; EBY = raw Bayer frame) ECM. Sequence ID (frames from the same observation, e.g. panorama tiles) NCAM00528. Captured 2026-09-17 07:38 UTC. Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech. [Source](https://mars.nasa.gov/mars2020/multimedia/raw-images/NLG_1982_0842901206_380ECM_N0910970NCAM00528_00_2I4J).

Each camera covers a 96 x 73 degree field of view at about 0.33 milliradians per pixel, downsampled for downlink from a native 5120x3840-pixel color sensor - sharp enough to make out an object the size of a golf ball 25 meters away. It's a normal wide-angle perspective, not a fisheye. Expect an ordinary-looking wide color landscape shot, often one tile of a larger panorama - frames sharing a sequence ID in the metadata are usually taken together as a mosaic.

## WATSON (SHERLOC's context camera) on Perseverance rover

<img src="{{ '/assets/img/2026-09-17/perseverance-SI1_1982_0842894867_558ECM_N0910970SRLC04004_0000LMJ.jpg' | absolute_url }}" alt="Perseverance SHERLOC_WATSON, sol 1982, filter OPEN">

*Perseverance SHERLOC_WATSON, sol 1982, filter OPEN.* Sol (Mars day of the mission) 1982. Filter OPEN. Product type (ECM = processed image; EBY = raw Bayer frame) ECM. Sequence ID (frames from the same observation) SRLC04004. Captured 2026-09-17 05:52 UTC. Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech. [Source](https://mars.nasa.gov/mars2020/multimedia/raw-images/SI1_1982_0842894867_558ECM_N0910970SRLC04004_0000LMJ).

A 1600x1200-pixel color sensor behind a fixed macro lens with no optical zoom; instead the arm moves the whole camera to change scale, focusing from about 2cm off the surface out to infinity. Close up it resolves roughly 14 micrometers per pixel, coarsening to around 100 micrometers per pixel farther back. Close-up, well-lit color shots of rock or soil texture, sometimes taken as a stack at slightly different distances or as a small mosaic; wider, more distant shots are usually rover selfies or arm/instrument inspections rather than science targets.

## Navcam (B-side pair) on Curiosity rover

<img src="{{ '/assets/img/2026-09-17/curiosity-NRB_842870288EDR_S1250198NCAM00594M_.jpg' | absolute_url }}" alt="Curiosity NAV_RIGHT_B, sol 5017">

*Curiosity NAV_RIGHT_B, sol 5017.* Sol (Mars day of the mission) 5017. Sequence ID (frames from the same observation) NCAM00594. Mast azimuth (degrees) 6.42367. Mast elevation (degrees) -11.7216. Local Mean Solar Time (Mars clock) Sol-05017M11:31:23.506. Captured 2026-09-16 23:50 UTC. Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech. [Source](https://mars.nasa.gov/msl-raw-images/proj/msl/redops/ods/surface/sol/05017/opgs/edr/ncam/NRB_842870288EDR_S1250198NCAM00594M_.JPG).

Monochrome images with a 45-degree field of view, paired with its left-eye partner (NAV_LEFT_B) for stereo depth; mounted just below the A-side pair on the mast, aimed the same direction. An ordinary-looking greyscale wide landscape shot, indistinguishable in content from the A-side navcams - only the instrument name in the metadata says which computer captured it.

## HiRISE (High Resolution Imaging Science Experiment) on Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter

<img src="{{ '/assets/img/2026-09-17/hirise-ESP_065221_2055.jpg' | absolute_url }}" alt="Layers in Flammarion Crater">

*Layers in Flammarion Crater.* Captured 2026-09-17 20:54 UTC. Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech/University of Arizona. [Source](https://uahirise.org/hipod/ESP_065221_2055).

From MRO's roughly 300km orbit, HiRISE resolves about 0.3 meters per pixel - individual boulders and rover tracks are visible. It scans a long strip as the spacecraft flies overhead; the full-width detector is black and white, but a narrower strip down the center is also imaged in three color bands (blue-green 400-600nm, red 550-850nm, near-infrared 800-1000nm). A full frame can run to about 20,000 x 126,000 pixels. A top-down, mostly greyscale strip of terrain, sharp enough at full resolution to show rocks, dunes and even rover hardware from orbit; color, when present, only runs down the central portion of the frame. What's published here is normally a cropped, downsized detail rather than a full multi-gigapixel swath.
