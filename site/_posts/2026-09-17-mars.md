## Mastcam-Z (ZCAM_L0_RGB) on Perseverance rover

<img src="{{ '/assets/img/2026-09-17/perseverance-ZL0_1982_0842891576_957ECM_N0910970ZCAM03022_100085J.jpg' | absolute_url }}" alt="Perseverance MCZ_LEFT, sol 1982, filter ZCAM_L0_RGB">

*Perseverance MCZ_LEFT, sol 1982, filter ZCAM_L0_RGB.* Sol (Mars day of the mission) 1982. Filter (e.g. ZCAM_L5_528NM; UNK/None means the Bayer color filter) ZCAM_L0_RGB. Product type (ECM = processed image; EBY = raw Bayer frame) ECM. Sequence ID (frames from the same observation) ZCAM03022. Captured 2026-09-17 04:57 UTC. Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech. [Source](https://mars.nasa.gov/mars2020/multimedia/raw-images/ZL0_1982_0842891576_957ECM_N0910970ZCAM03022_100085J).

Each eye has an 8-position filter wheel behind the zoom lens, producing 1600x1200-pixel frames. Position 0 (L0/R0) is a Bayer color filter for ordinary RGB images. Positions 1-6 are narrowband science filters: the left eye at 800, 754, 677, 605, 528 and 442nm, the right eye at 800, 866, 910, 939, 978 and 1022nm, spanning visible light into the near infrared. Position 7 on each eye is a neutral-density filter for looking directly at the sun; those frames are excluded from this feed. Most raw frames look like an ordinary color landscape photo (the L0/R0 Bayer filter). A narrowband frame (filter name like ZCAM_L5_528NM) is instead a flat greyscale image of a single wavelength - that's expected, not a bug; several such frames sharing a sequence ID are meant to be stacked later into a false-color composite. Frames sharing a sequence ID can also just be tiles of a wider mosaic or panorama.

## Navcam (B-side pair) on Curiosity rover

<img src="{{ '/assets/img/2026-09-17/curiosity-NRB_842870063EDR_S1250198NCAM00594M_.jpg' | absolute_url }}" alt="Curiosity NAV_RIGHT_B, sol 5017">

*Curiosity NAV_RIGHT_B, sol 5017.* Sol (Mars day of the mission) 5017. Sequence ID (frames from the same observation) NCAM00594. Mast azimuth (degrees) 185.809. Mast elevation (degrees) -1.57532. Local Mean Solar Time (Mars clock) Sol-05017M11:27:44.646. Captured 2026-09-16 23:46 UTC. Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech. [Source](https://mars.nasa.gov/msl-raw-images/proj/msl/redops/ods/surface/sol/05017/opgs/edr/ncam/NRB_842870063EDR_S1250198NCAM00594M_.JPG).

Monochrome images with a 45-degree field of view, paired with its left-eye partner (NAV_LEFT_B) for stereo depth; mounted just below the A-side pair on the mast, aimed the same direction. An ordinary-looking greyscale wide landscape shot, indistinguishable in content from the A-side navcams - only the instrument name in the metadata says which computer captured it.

## HiRISE (High Resolution Imaging Science Experiment) on Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter

<img src="{{ '/assets/img/2026-09-17/hirise-ESP_065221_2055.jpg' | absolute_url }}" alt="Layers in Flammarion Crater">

*Layers in Flammarion Crater.* Captured 2026-09-17 17:47 UTC. Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech/University of Arizona. [Source](https://uahirise.org/hipod/ESP_065221_2055).

From MRO's roughly 300km orbit, HiRISE resolves about 0.3 meters per pixel - individual boulders and rover tracks are visible. It scans a long strip as the spacecraft flies overhead; the full-width detector is black and white, but a narrower strip down the center is also imaged in three color bands (blue-green 400-600nm, red 550-850nm, near-infrared 800-1000nm). A full frame can run to about 20,000 x 126,000 pixels. A top-down, mostly greyscale strip of terrain, sharp enough at full resolution to show rocks, dunes and even rover hardware from orbit; color, when present, only runs down the central portion of the frame. What's published here is normally a cropped, downsized detail rather than a full multi-gigapixel swath.
